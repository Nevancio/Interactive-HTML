World-Class Experience Characteristics

A System Transforming Against the Grain of Car Culture

LA Metro operates in the most challenging environment of any system in this benchmark: a sprawling, car-centric metropolitan area of 10 million people where transit has historically been an afterthought of urban planning. Despite this structural disadvantage, LA Metro has embarked on one of the most ambitious transit expansion programs in US history — adding 100+ miles of rail and BRT lines in preparation for the 2028 LA Olympics. The scale of this transformation, funded primarily by dedicated sales tax revenue, is reshaping what transit means in Los Angeles.

LA Metro's GoPass program — offering integrated ticketing across 20+ transit agencies in the LA region — is one of the most ambitious multi-agency fare integration initiatives in the US. While still maturing, GoPass represents the kind of regional cooperation that NCDOT's independent operators — GoRaleigh, GoTriangle, GoCary, GoDurham, CATS, Greensboro Transit, and others — need to achieve statewide. Note: North Carolina already uses "GoPass" branding for the GoTriangle regional fare product (built on UMO); the LA Metro GoPass is a separate initiative. Source: GoTriangle GoPass/UMO

Technology Stack

TAP card system accepted across Metro, municipal bus operators, and Metrolink commuter rail. GoLA app consolidating multi-modal journey planning. GTFS-RT feeds for rail and bus. GoPass multi-agency integrated ticketing pilot across 20+ operators. Express Lanes dynamic pricing data integration feeding into journey planner. LA28 Olympics infrastructure upgrades accelerating digital modernization.

User Experience

TAP card's multi-agency acceptance is the system's strongest UX asset — a single card works across Metro rail, Metro bus, and many municipal operators. The GoLA app is improving but remains inconsistent in real-time data quality. LA Metro's station environments are generally modern and well-maintained on newer lines (Expo, Crenshaw), though older stations show deferred maintenance. Wayfinding quality varies significantly by line age.

First/Last Mile Challenge

LA Metro's single largest UX challenge is the first/last mile gap. In a car-centric city with low-density land use patterns, getting to and from transit stations without a car remains difficult for most potential riders. The absence of deep e-scooter, e-bike, and rideshare integration in the GoLA app means riders face a fragmented multi-app experience for complete journey planning.

Opportunity Areas

Where LA Metro Must Improve

  • Upgrade TAP to open-loop contactless EMV acceptance — requiring a proprietary agency card creates onboarding friction for tourists and occasional riders, especially given LA's global tourism industry.
  • Deepen first/last mile integration: e-scooter, e-bike, and rideshare booking must be seamlessly embedded in GoLA to address LA's unique low-density geography.
  • Build real-time crowding data into GoLA — this data gap is particularly painful in LA where riders often avoid transit due to uncertainty about crowding conditions.
  • Complete GoPass rollout and expand to cover all LA basin operators — the 20+ agency fragmentation is the primary data silo problem LA Metro must solve.
  • Develop a post-journey loyalty and sustainability tracking layer to reward consistent transit use over car travel.
Centricity on Customer Needs

Building a Transit Culture in a Car Capital

LA Metro's customer centricity challenge is unlike any other system in this benchmark: it must persuade a population that has built its entire lifestyle around automobile use to consider transit as a genuine alternative. This requires not just a good app — it requires a fundamentally different value proposition than what transit typically offers. LA Metro has responded with free fare programs (Metro Micro, student ridership programs), high-frequency service on key corridors, and a focus on station design that projects confidence rather than necessity.

The Olympics acceleration effect is real: LA28 is driving federal investment in transit infrastructure, improved airport connections, and digital experience upgrades that would otherwise take a decade to achieve. LA Metro is explicitly using the Olympic deadline as a forcing function to compress transformation timelines. North Carolina has no single equivalent mega-event, but the Research Triangle and Charlotte metro areas are among the fastest-growing in the United States — population pressure, traffic congestion, and corporate relocations (notably the Research Triangle Park ecosystem) provide a sustained forcing function that can be used similarly to advocate for statewide transit investment.

Key Gap: LA Metro's GoLA app UX lags significantly behind what a car-replacement transit experience requires. For transit to compete with driving in LA, the app experience needs to be not just equivalent to car navigation apps — it needs to be demonstrably better. Currently, Google Maps provides a superior multimodal journey experience in LA than LA Metro's own digital properties.

Commerce & Revenue Generation

Sales Tax Innovation — The US Model for Dedicated Transit Funding

LA Metro's revenue model is notable for one thing above all others: dedicated sales tax funding through voter-approved ballot measures. Measure M (2016) and Measure R (2008) together generate approximately $2 billion annually in dedicated transit capital and operating funding, insulating LA Metro from annual budget battles that constrain agencies like NCDOT's supported operators and WMATA. This is arguably the single most directly applicable revenue model for North Carolina: Charlotte/Mecklenburg County already operates a transit sales tax supporting CATS — the CATS model is NC's closest existing analog to Measure M. Source: CATS Transit Funding Report (NCDOT/NC FIRST)

Fare Revenue Sales Tax (Measure M + R) Federal/State Grants Express Lanes Tolls Transit-Oriented Development Advertising

Express Lanes tolls on I-10 and I-110 generate direct revenue while simultaneously making transit more competitive by slowing adjacent freeway traffic. North Carolina's I-77 South Express Lanes (Charlotte area) and I-540 (Raleigh/Triangle) are directly analogous toll infrastructure that could similarly be structured to direct a portion of toll revenue to regional transit capital. Sources: I-77 South Express Lanes (NCDOT) · NC Turnpike Authority FY2025 Budget. Transit-oriented development partnerships — building residential and commercial projects above and adjacent to stations — are generating long-term ground lease and sale revenue, a model applicable to Raleigh Union Station, Charlotte Center City Transit Station, and Durham Station in NC.

Fare recovery ~18% as cited in this benchmark — verify against LA Metro FY2023/24 audited financials. Measure M alone generates approximately $860 million per year in dedicated funding. Notable Innovation

GoPass: Multi-Agency Integration at Scale

LA Metro's GoPass initiative — integrating TAP card acceptance across 20+ independent transit operators in the LA basin — is the most ambitious multi-agency fare integration project in the US outside of the DC region's SmarTrip program. GoPass directly addresses the fragmentation problem: a rider traveling from the San Fernando Valley via a municipal bus, Metro rail, and a coastal shuttle currently needs multiple fare instruments. GoPass collapses these into one. The technical and political challenges of achieving this across 20 independent agencies in car-centric LA make it an underappreciated achievement — and a direct model for what North Carolina needs: a statewide version of GoPass that spans the Triangle (GoTriangle, GoCary, GoDurham), Charlotte (CATS), Greensboro, and intercity Piedmont/Carolinian rail connections. The Triangle's GoTriangle GoPass/UMO pilot is already the NC seed of this model. Source: GoTriangle GoPass/UMO
