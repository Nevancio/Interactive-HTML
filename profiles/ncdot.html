World-Class Experience Characteristics

Federated Without Standardization — The Core Challenge, Amplified

NCDOT's Public Transportation Division (PTD) coordinates one of the most fragmented transit landscapes of any US state — dozens of independent operators, each with its own apps, fare media, data systems, and technology stacks, unified only by NCDOT state funding and the Uniform Public Transportation Accounting System (UPTAS). Where other systems in this benchmark have silos within a single operator, North Carolina has silos between entirely separate agencies: GoRaleigh, GoTriangle, CATS (Charlotte), GoDurham, GoCary, Greensboro Transit Agency, Asheville, Wilmington, and others each operate independently.

The state's transit landscape spans bus (local and intercity), commuter rail (state-supported Piedmont and Carolinian Amtrak services), ferry (NC Ferry System), and toll roads (NC Turnpike Authority — I-77, I-540). A rider traveling from Cary to Durham touches GoCary, GoTriangle, and possibly GoDurham — three separate fare systems, three separate apps, and zero integrated journey data. The North Carolina Research Triangle and Charlotte are among the fastest-growing metro areas in the United States, creating urgent but unmet demand for integrated mobility.

Technology Stack

No statewide unified technology platform exists. Individual operators have varying levels of digital maturity: GoTriangle has deployed the UMO mobile ticketing platform and the GoPass regional fare product (covering Triangle-area operators); CATS has its own app; GoRaleigh and GoDurham operate separately. Real-time GTFS-RT feeds exist for some operators but are not aggregated into a statewide open API. No unified journey planner, no statewide contactless payment standard, and no open data portal exists at the NCDOT level. Sources: GoTriangle GoPass/UMO · GoRaleigh

User Experience

Each operator provides a separate rider experience. GoTriangle's UMO/GoPass pilot represents the most advanced regional integration in the state, allowing Triangle-area riders to use a single digital payment across participating operators. Outside the Triangle, riders face entirely separate systems. There is no equivalent of Singapore's MyTransport.SG, Zürich's SBB Mobile, or even WMATA's SmarTrip across the full state. A statewide visitor or commuter crossing operator boundaries must maintain multiple fare accounts with no wayfinding integration connecting them.

Structural Dependency

NCDOT's unique structural challenge — coordinating dozens of independent operators without a mandate to operate them directly — means that digitization cannot be top-down mandated in the way London TfL or Singapore LTA can deploy new technology. Every upgrade requires multi-operator political consensus, separate procurement processes, and individual operator capacity to absorb new systems. This is the root cause of data silo issues that no single technology deployment can fully resolve without governance changes at the state coordination level. Source: NCDOT PTD operator list

Centricity on Customer Needs

The Multi-App, Multi-Account Problem: A NC Rider's Daily Reality

The clearest measure of North Carolina's transit coordination gap is what a typical Research Triangle commuter must do to complete a single cross-operator trip. They open a separate app for each operator — GoTriangle, GoCary, and GoDurham each have separate platforms. They load separate fare balances on separate cards or apps. They encounter no unified wayfinding connecting these systems. They board with no information about connecting service timing at transfer points. They complete their journey with no record of it, no loyalty credit, and no mechanism for integrated feedback.

The GoTriangle GoPass/UMO pilot is a genuine step forward for the Triangle region — it demonstrates that regional integration is technically feasible in NC. But it remains limited in geographic scope. Charlotte Area Transit System (CATS) operates separately. Greensboro, Asheville, Wilmington, and other operators have no integration with Triangle systems. The statewide picture remains fully fragmented.

Benchmark Comparison: Singapore LTA riders complete their entire journey — plan, pay, board, transfer, review — in one app across one unified system. GoTriangle Triangle-area riders can now use GoPass across Triangle operators, which is genuine progress. But North Carolina as a whole still faces the multi-operator coordination challenge that MTA/OMNY addresses at scale — except multiplied across dozens of fully independent agencies rather than one federated system. The coordination gap is structural, not just technological.

Commerce & Revenue Generation

Fragmented Revenue — A Structural Vulnerability Across All Operators

NCDOT's financial model — aggregated across all state-supported operators — is the most fragmented of any system in this benchmark. Each operator runs an independently subsidy-dependent model: approximately 65–75% state/federal funding, 20–30% fares, and less than 5% non-fare revenue. This near-identical pattern across dozens of operators reflects the absence of any statewide revenue coordination strategy. Fare recovery data is tracked through the Uniform Public Transportation Accounting System (UPTAS) [TO BE VERIFIED for all figures]. Source: NCDOT UPTAS

No Statewide Data Licensing Revenue No Unified Loyalty Program No Statewide Retail Partnerships No Coordinated Property Development

North Carolina's transit operators collectively have virtually none of the coordinated non-fare revenue streams that sustain peer systems. There is no statewide data licensing program, no unified loyalty platform, no coordinated station retail strategy, and no transit-oriented development initiative comparable to WMATA's joint development program or JR East's real estate division. Because operators are independent, none can achieve the transaction volume or data licensing scale that Tokyo, Singapore, or London have built.

Revenue Opportunity: North Carolina's major transit hubs — Raleigh Union Station (GoRaleigh), Charlotte Center City Transit Station (CATS), and Durham Station (GoDurham) — represent underutilized real estate and transit-oriented development assets. A coordinated NCDOT Joint Development program, modeled on WMATA's 20+ TOD projects, could generate significant non-fare revenue without fare increases. The GoTriangle GoPass/UMO platform could be scaled statewide to become a payments and data infrastructure generating transaction fees analogous to Seoul's T-money ecosystem.

Key Context: NC's combined transit operating budget, if aggregated across all NCDOT-supported operators, is estimated at $1.5–2.5B annually [TO BE VERIFIED — source: NCDOT PTD budget; individual operator UPTAS reports]. Fare recovery averages approximately 20–25% statewide [TO BE VERIFIED]. There is no NC equivalent of NYC's congestion pricing, London's ULEZ, or LA's Measure M dedicated sales tax — making NC transit entirely dependent on year-to-year state appropriations and federal formula grants.

Pre-Transit: GoTriangle's GoPass/UMO pilot provides partial regional integration in the Triangle — this is a genuine innovation that demonstrates feasibility. However, statewide pre-trip planning across all NC operators requires multiple separate apps. No unified NC transit journey planner exists. CATS, Greensboro Transit, GoRaleigh, and others are each planned via separate digital channels or third-party apps (Google Maps, Transit App). Source: GoTriangle GoPass/UMO

During Transit: Some NC operators publish GTFS-RT feeds but there is no statewide aggregation or unified real-time API. Service alerts are operator-specific with no guaranteed cross-operator delivery. Real-time train positions for Piedmont/Carolinian are Amtrak-managed. There is no NC equivalent of TfL's open API ecosystem powering 700+ third-party apps.

Post-Transit: No post-journey data layer exists in any form at any NC operator at scale. There is no unified journey history, no statewide loyalty program, no personalization, and no AI recommendations. Every journey NC's transit riders complete disappears from any coordinated system memory the moment the vehicle arrives.

The Core Problem: North Carolina sits in the worst possible position: federated without standardization. It has all the complexity of a multi-operator environment — dozens of agencies with separate governance, contracts, and systems — with none of the standardization benefits that Switzerland, Tokyo, or even Washington DC have achieved through regional coordination agreements. The WMATA SmarTrip model (voluntary multi-agency acceptance via a regional compact) and the GoTriangle GoPass pilot are the closest available blueprints for what NC needs to replicate at scale.

Notable Innovation

GoPass/UMO — Early Regional Integration in the Triangle

GoTriangle's GoPass platform — built on the UMO mobile ticketing system — is the most significant transit technology innovation in North Carolina to date. It provides Triangle-area riders with a single digital payment credential accepted across participating operators (GoTriangle, GoCary, GoDurham), demonstrating that voluntary multi-operator integration is achievable in NC without a single consolidated operator. This is the seed of what NC needs statewide. Source: GoTriangle fares & passes

CATS in Charlotte operates light rail, buses, and paratransit with dedicated funding from Charlotte/Mecklenburg County's transit sales tax — the closest NC analog to LA Metro's Measure M model. The CATS model demonstrates that dedicated, locally-controlled transit funding is achievable in NC. The NC FIRST Commission's CATS transit funding report provides further context on replicating and scaling this model. Source: CATS Transit Funding Report (NCDOT/NC FIRST)

The Path Forward: NCDOT's position as the lowest-ranked system in this benchmark is also its greatest strategic opportunity. Every system ranked above it offers a proven blueprint. Singapore's open API model, Zürich's federated standardization across 250+ operators, London's open data revolution, WMATA's SmarTrip regional compact, and LA Metro's dedicated sales tax funding are all directly applicable to NC's situation. The GoTriangle GoPass pilot, the CATS dedicated funding model, and the NC Turnpike Authority's toll infrastructure provide existing foundations to build on. The barrier is not technology — it is statewide governance, inter-operator coordination, and sustained capital commitment.
